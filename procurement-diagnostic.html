The challenge

A function built for your ambitions, not inherited by accident

When structure grows by accident the result is misaligned roles, unclear accountabilities, duplicated effort and missed value. The executive team senses it but cannot see it clearly.

01

Unclear mandate and influence

Procurement is seen as a compliance step or a bottleneck rather than a partner, and is brought in too late to shape outcomes.

02

Structure that does not fit the business

Roles, reporting lines and decision rights reflect history rather than where spend and risk actually sit.

03

Processes that slow everyone down

Frameworks built for probity become complex enough that stakeholders work around them, increasing risk rather than reducing it.

Operating model options

There is no single best structure, only the right one for your strategy

The right model depends on where decisions are made, how diverse your business units are and how your organisation defines value. We test the options with your leadership team rather than arriving with a template.

Decentralised

Each business unit runs its own buying, with little shared category work.

Coordinated

A small central team sets policy, while category and sourcing work stays with the business units.

Centre-led

The centre owns category strategy and panels, and business units buy locally within them.

Centralised

One team owns strategy, sourcing and contracts for all addressable spend.
